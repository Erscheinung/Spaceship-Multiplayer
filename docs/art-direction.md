# Afterlight Skyway visual direction

An inhabited orbital city seen from a little independent courier ship, with NEON WING’s cool painted surfaces and luminous courier-street accents. The city should feel like a place with businesses, routines and history. The visual target is illustrated architecture with material depth and intentional detail, rather than a collection of random colored boxes.

The Gemini concepts in the user's local Sunset Town `docs/concept` directory informed the approach: `city-old-town-street-gemini-2026-11-04.png` for occupied windows and layered storefronts, `texture-swatches-gemini-2026-11-03.png` for restrained material wear, and `city-docks-dusk-gemini-2026-11-06.jpg` for cool evening atmosphere with warm working lights. These are reference images, not shipped game assets.

Buildings combine plaster and selected masonry, substantial cornices, shop plinths, inset framed panes, timber, striped awnings and small balcony planters. The local business kit has eight original illustrated fascias: Afterlight Post, Comet Coffee, Nightjar Radio, Moss & Moon, Ring Road Repairs, Blue Hour Books, Lunar Noodle Club and The Starling Hotel. Their pictograms, typography, pinstripes and short lines are composed into [one 512 × 512 atlas](screenshots/graphics-storefronts.png). Existing travel posters remain part of the city's identity.

The near city is built from individual properties rather than a single long repeated slab. Six construction families combine different forms and materials: brick bay-window houses with iron balconies, indigo timber noodle shops with counters/lanterns/noren, honey-stone arcades, glazed teal ceramic cafés, shuttered rose townhouses, and sage workshops. Heights, setbacks, opening shapes, frontage depth, framing and roof construction change together. Avoid a common end-wall template with a different roof attached. Large, readable differences in construction take priority over dense decorative geometry.

Ships retain their clear delta silhouette and pilot colors, with bevelled hull edges, clean panel seams, wing trim, a reflective framed canopy, engine mouths and an asymmetric courier marking. Keep wear restrained on the ship so it remains the visual focus.

The material kit in `surfaces.js` uses a [shared illustrated sixteen-surface atlas](texture-assets.md) for plaster, masonry, timber, paving, roofs, ceramics and fabric, alongside window, lamp, painted metal and fascia finishes. The palette coordinates ivory, rose, teal, sage and deep indigo, with cyan, coral and violet reserved for readable neon signs and small fixtures. Broad painted plaster and smooth midnight-teal asphalt stay quiet; ceramics, roofs and timber retain stronger construction patterns. Compress atlas contrast by material rather than washing every surface in grain or multiplying earthy albedo into the scene. Keep the six construction families distinct through silhouette, joinery and openings. Face coordinates are captured before merging; road coordinates follow absolute route distance. Correct colour conversion, padded cells, isolated mip levels and metric scale are required so the surfaces retain their character without distant shimmer or unrelated tile colours bleeding together.

Surface variety comes from authored timber, quiet plaster, glazed ceramic, stone, roofs and cloth. The Messenger reference (https://messenger.abeto.co/) informs the cohesive teal/ivory color relationships, broad tonal shapes and intentional surface detail; its assets are not copied. Texture detail must support the architecture without competing with it. Smooth road surfaces should reveal clear ship and building shadows. A shared 1024 × 1024 GPU atlas replaces those structural surface patterns; no additional fullscreen pass or individual light is needed. Impact effects use short radial sparks rather than expanding wire spheres that could be mistaken for solid hazards.

Lighting defaults to the **device's local civil clock**, including its timezone/DST rules. Dawn is centered on 06:00 and dusk on 18:00 with gradual blue-hour/golden transitions. Auto, Dawn, Day, Golden hour and Night are available in the terminal and pause panel; a saved override is cosmetic and local to each pilot. This is an art-directed clock cycle, not astronomical sunrise/sunset: latitude, season and geolocation are not inferred from a timezone. Different-zone multiplayer pilots may see different skies; simulation and network time are unaffected. Production lighting refreshes once per second and catches up after tab suspension.

A single sky sphere supplies a sun disk, halo and restrained angular rays; the opposite moon and sparse stars appear at night. These are sky radiance, not volumetric shafts between buildings. Architecture naturally occludes them. One directional shadow light and the existing hemisphere/bloom/FXAA pipeline remain; night windows and lanterns use emissive surfaces, not individual lights.

Keep the existing resolution cap, shadow map size, instance counts and rolling updates. Sign and material atlases are shared with scene-level ownership and disposed when the last scene closes; late image completion must not revive disposed textures. Other materials/geometry keep their existing object ownership. Do not add per-frame textures, extra fullscreen effects or individual window lights. Continuous ground must follow the sampled road frame through turns, hills and banks, covering visible gaps between properties. Validate actual gameplay and inspect day, golden-hour, dawn, night and portrait captures; measure against the previous renderer on the same device. Software WebGL establishes correctness and relative cost, not phone/Safari GPU performance.
